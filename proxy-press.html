Printer settings guide

Cutting machine

For a Silhouette Cameo: turn on registration marks under Layout → Silhouette Cameo, print the PDF, then load this file in Silhouette Studio.

Cutting with a rotary trimmer

No Silhouette Cameo? A rotary paper trimmer (for example a Dahle 507) and a 3 mm corner rounder cut cards just as cleanly. It takes about 2 minutes per sheet once you're used to it.

1 · Set up the sheet

  • Use Margin lines + corners for cut guides. Margin lines guide the first cuts, and corner marks stay on every strip after the sheet is cut apart.
  • Use as much bleed as fits, with a 0 mm gap: 2 mm on landscape 4×2, 1 mm on portrait 3×3. A cut can be off by that much without showing a white edge.
  • Print the Calibration sheet first so the marks land exactly where the PDF says.

2 · Before you cut

  • Let the sheet dry. Laser prints are ready right away. Inkjet needs at least 10 minutes, and overnight is better if the ProxyJak stock feels soft.
  • Trim a scrap first and check which edge of the cut-line indicator the blade actually cuts on.
  • Cut face up so you can see the marks, and hold the sheet flat under the clamp bar.

Where to line up the blade

  • Margin ticks are thin black lines in the page margin, one at every card edge. They show where the first cuts go.
  • Corner marks are short lines at every card corner, in the bleed. They stay on each strip after the margins are gone.
  • Every cut goes exactly through a line. Put the line under your trimmer's cutting indicator (the thin guide strip or line on the rail) at both ends before you cut.
  • Turn on Show cut lines above the preview to see every cut as a red dashed line. It only shows on screen, not in the print.

3 · Cut the columns

  1. Line the blade up with the tick marks in the top and bottom margins. Check both ends before every cut.
  2. Trim the left margin off, then work across the sheet. Between two cards there are two lines, one for each card's edge, with both cards' bleed in between. Cut both and throw away the bleed strip.
  3. You now have one tall strip per column.

4 · Cut the rows

  1. Turn each strip 90° and line the blade up with the corner marks on both sides of the strip.
  2. Again, cut both lines between cards and throw away the bleed strip.
  3. Cut one strip at a time. Stacked strips drift, and black-core stock can split if forced.

5 · Finish

  • Hold the first card against a real card. If it's off, fix your blade alignment before cutting the rest.
  • Round the corners. See Corner cutting below.
  • Sleeve the cards. Sleeves hide small size differences, and the ProxyJak inkjet stock needs them because it has no varnish.
  • Fuzzy or torn edges mean the blade is dull. Rotate or replace it.
  • Double-sided sheets: print the backs before cutting. All cuts follow the front marks.

Corner cutting

Real Magic cards have corners of roughly 3 mm (about 1/8 in) radius. Pick a tool that matches that.

Your tool: Sun-Star Kadomaru Pro
  • Use the 3 mm slot (the smallest). 5 mm and 8 mm are too round.
  • One card per punch. Proxy stock is thicker than it's rated for, so press firmly and evenly.
  • Test on an offcut first. If it crushes or won't cut cleanly, the Kadomaru Pro Neo is rated for 0.4 mm.
  • Empty the scrap tray in the base often, or corners stop cutting cleanly.
Corner rounder punch, R3 / 3 mmCheapest and most common. Best for a few cards at a time. Skip R2 (too square) and R4/R5 (too round).
Heavy-duty desktop corner cutterA lever cutter with fixed guides. Worth it for 300gsm and heavier stock, black core, or hundreds of cards.
Card die cutterCuts the whole card, corners included, in one press. Fastest for big batches, but you have to line up the die with the print.
Fine nail file / 400+ gritTouch-ups only. Smooths a small nub or a slightly uneven corner.
  • Round corners after trimming cards to their final size and before sleeving.
  • Practise on the bleed slivers and offcuts from trimming before you touch a real card.
  • Push the card firmly into both guide walls for every punch. Most lopsided corners come from a card that wasn't fully seated.
  • Punch face up. The blade pushes any burr to the back of the card, where it won't show.
  • On 300gsm+ and black-core stock, punch one card at a time. Stacks drift, and the layers can split.
  • Turn the card the same way after each punch (for example clockwise) so all four corners come out the same.
  • Inkjet prints must be fully dry, or the coating can crack or smudge at the corner.
  • Fuzzy edges, a visible core, or split layers mean the blade is dull. Clean out the coating build-up, punch a few times through wax paper to lubricate it, or replace the blade.
  • Check your first card against a real one. The corners should sit exactly on top of each other.
